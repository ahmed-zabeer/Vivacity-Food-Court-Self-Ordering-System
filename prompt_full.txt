# FEATURE UPDATE: LIVE FOOD PREPARATION ANIMATION + COUNTER-CONFIRMED SOUND REMINDER

You are working on my existing web application:

**VIVA CITY FOOD COURT**

Implement a complete, interactive food preparation tracking and customer notification feature inside the existing application.

IMPORTANT:

* Do not rebuild the entire application.
* Do not change the existing brand, menu data, payment system, 55-table QR system, or core ordering architecture.
* Integrate this feature with the existing customer app, central management dashboard, stall dashboards, and kitchen display system.
* This must be a functional feature connected to real order states, not just an animation or static UI mockup.

The main goal is to give customers a premium, attractive experience that shows their food being prepared, tells them approximately how long it may take, shows exactly which stall's food is ready, and plays a pleasant sound reminder when the central counter confirms that the food is ready for collection.

---

## 1. CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE IMMEDIATELY AFTER PLACING AN ORDER

When a customer places an order, show a beautiful, animated order-tracking screen instead of a plain success message.

Example:

**Order Received!**

Your food journey has started.

Display:

* Customer order token, such as #0048
* Table number, such as T17
* Order time
* Ordered items
* Stall names
* Current order status
* Estimated preparation time when available

Use an elegant chef-themed animation showing a chef preparing food.

The animation could include a chef cooking, subtle steam, a moving pan, or food preparation motions. Use a lightweight Lottie animation, suitable animation asset, or tasteful CSS animation.

The animation must look modern and premium, not childish.

Show a message such as:

"Your order is being prepared with care."

However, respect the actual order workflow.

Before management acceptance, payment confirmation, and order release, display the correct status, such as:

"Waiting for order confirmation"

or

"Preparing your order request"

Do not falsely claim that the kitchen has started preparing food before the order has actually been released to the relevant stall.

Once the order has been accepted, the required payment has been confirmed, and the kitchen has received the order, update the message to:

**"Our chefs are preparing your food!"**

The preparation animation should then become active.

## 2. SHOW EACH STALL'S STATUS SEPARATELY

A customer may place one order containing food from multiple stalls.

For example:

Order #0048
Table T17

* Coffee Buzz Fusion Cafe: T Bone Steak
* Momo Buzz: Chicken Momo
* Burger Bar: Chicken Cheese Delight

These must remain one master order from the customer's perspective.

However, display the preparation status of each stall separately.

Create an attractive section titled:

**Your Food Preparation Status**

Example:

### Coffee Buzz Fusion Cafe

T Bone Steak × 1

Status: PREPARING

Show:

* Animated chef preparing food
* Estimated remaining time, such as 8 minutes
* A preparation progress indicator

### Momo Buzz

Chicken Momo × 2

Status: READY — AWAITING COUNTER CONFIRMATION

Show that the stall has finished preparing the food, but the central counter has not yet confirmed the customer notification.

### Burger Bar

Chicken Cheese Delight × 1

Status: WAITING / PREPARING

Show the chef animation when preparation has started.

The customer must immediately understand:

* Which stall is preparing food
* Which stall has finished
* Which stall is still pending
* Which ready items are awaiting counter confirmation
* Whether the customer has been notified to collect ready food

Never display the entire master order as READY if even one stall's items are still pending.

## 3. DYNAMIC ESTIMATED PREPARATION TIME

Display an estimated preparation time separately for every stall in a multi-stall order.

Examples:

Coffee Buzz Fusion Cafe
"Approximately 8 minutes remaining"

Momo Buzz
"Approximately 2 minutes remaining"

Burger Bar
"Estimated 10 minutes"

These values are illustrative. Use actual configured preparation times and order-state information where available.

Add a preparation-time setting to the existing menu item or stall configuration so authorized management can configure an estimated preparation time.

For example:

T Bone Steak: 15 minutes

Chicken Momo: 8 minutes

Waffle: 6 minutes

If an order contains multiple items from the same stall, calculate the stall's estimated preparation duration using appropriate configurable business logic. Do not simply add every item's preparation time together unless the existing kitchen workflow explicitly requires sequential preparation.

The estimated time should update when:

* A stall starts preparation
* A kitchen staff member updates the status
* The preparation estimate changes
* The stall marks its food ready

When the remaining time reaches zero but the food is not marked ready, do not automatically claim the food is ready. Display an appropriate message, such as:

"Taking a little longer than expected"

When the estimate is unavailable, show:

"Preparation time will be updated shortly"

Do not display misleading, randomly generated countdowns.

## 4. CENTRAL COUNTER DASHBOARD: READY-FOOD CONFIRMATION

This is the most important part of the feature.

When kitchen staff finish an order at a stall, they press:

**MARK READY**

The corresponding stall dashboard and kitchen display update to READY.

The central management/counter dashboard must receive this update immediately.

Create a prominent section:

**Ready Food — Awaiting Counter Confirmation**

Example:

TOKEN #0048

TABLE T17

Ready stall: Momo Buzz

Items:

* Chicken Momo × 2

Kitchen Status: READY

Customer Notification: NOT SENT

Display two actions:

**CONFIRM & NOTIFY CUSTOMER**

**VIEW ORDER**

The counter staff must be able to identify the correct table and order before notifying the customer.

When counter staff clicks "CONFIRM & NOTIFY CUSTOMER":

1. Validate that the relevant stall items are actually marked ready.
2. Record the confirmation against the correct order, table, and stall.
3. Update the customer-facing status to "Ready for Pickup".
4. Play a pleasant sound reminder on the customer's active web app when the browser permits it.
5. Display a prominent visual notification on the customer's screen.
6. Show exactly which stall's food is ready.
7. Keep other stalls' preparation statuses unchanged.
8. Record the notification and confirmation timestamps.
9. Prevent accidental duplicate notifications from repeated clicks or page refreshes.

Do not send a ready-food notification merely because a kitchen staff member clicked READY. The customer-facing sound reminder must be triggered by the authorized counter confirmation.

The counter dashboard must always distinguish between:

* Preparing
* Kitchen Ready
* Awaiting Counter Confirmation
* Customer Notified
* Collected / Completed

Use clear status badges and an easy-to-understand workflow.

## 5. BEAUTIFUL CUSTOMER SOUND REMINDER

After counter confirmation, play a pleasant, premium notification sound on the customer's device.

The sound should be:

* Clear and recognizable
* Short and pleasant
* Suitable for a modern restaurant
* Not frightening or excessively loud
* Not similar to an emergency alarm

Prefer a short, elegant two-note chime or a similarly tasteful notification sound.

Use a bundled sound asset or a suitable browser-generated tone. Avoid relying on a broken external audio URL.

When the counter confirms that Momo Buzz's food is ready, show a notification like:

**Your Food Is Ready!**

Momo Buzz has finished preparing your food.

Table: T17

Token: #0048

**Please collect your ready food from the pickup counter.**

Use the actual stall name, table number, order token, and ready items from the database. Never hard-code T17 or #0048.

If Coffee Buzz Fusion Cafe finishes later, the next notification must identify that stall and its ready items accurately.

### Sound Reliability

Modern browsers can restrict automatic audio playback until the customer has interacted with the page.

Prepare the notification system accordingly:

* Initialize audio capability after the customer interacts with the ordering app.
* Play the sound when a valid counter-confirmed ready event arrives and playback is permitted.
* Show the visual notification even if sound playback is blocked.
* Provide a clear sound status or notification preference control where necessary.
* Do not play the same notification repeatedly after a page refresh.
* Do not replay an old notification simply because the customer reopened the order page.
* Avoid playing multiple overlapping sounds for the same confirmation.

The notification should work when the customer is actively viewing the web app. If the app is in the background or closed, use browser notification or web-push capability only where correctly configured and permission has been granted. Never promise that a sound will play when the browser or device prevents it.

For example:

Momo Buzz is ready, while Coffee Buzz Fusion Cafe is still preparing food.

The customer may collect the Momo Buzz items after the counter confirms them.

On the tracking screen, display a clear section:

**Ready to Collect**

* Momo Buzz
* Chicken Momo × 2

**Still Being Prepared**

* Burger Bar — Chicken Cheese Delight

The customer should not have to wait for all stalls to finish before learning that some food is ready.

However, the overall master order remains in progress until all required items have reached their appropriate completion state.

Track collection at the individual stall or ready-item group level where appropriate. Do not mark all of the master order as delivered or completed just because one stall's food has been collected.

Keep each ready/collection action linked to the correct table, token, order, and stall.
